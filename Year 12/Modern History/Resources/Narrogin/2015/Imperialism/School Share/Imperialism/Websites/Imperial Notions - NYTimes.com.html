    Imperial Notions

    By Michelle Sale and Javaid Khan
    June 4, 2003 12:52 pmJune 4, 2003 12:52 pm

    Note: This lesson was originally published on an older version of The Learning Network; the link to the related Times article will take you to a page on the old site.


    Teaching ideas based on New York Times content.

    • See all lesson plans »

    Overview of Lesson Plan: In this lesson, students research how and why different parts of the world were colonized, considering the pros and cons for both the rulers and the ruled.

    Author(s):

    Michelle Sale, The New York Times Learning Network

    Javaid Khan, the Bank Street College of Education in New York

    Suggested Time Allowance: 1 hour

    Objectives:
    Students will:
    1. Consider definitions for “colonialism” and “imperialism,” then use prior knowledge to brainstorm lists of countries involved in these practices.
    2. Discover how citizens of the Indian state of Goa plan to use their colonial past to attract tourists by reading and discussing “India Dusts Off Colonial Past, Says Come to Goa.”
    3. In groups, research how and why different parts of the world were colonized, considering the pros and cons for both the rulers and the ruled; create oral presentations with the information gathered through this research.
    4. Individually, express clear opinions on “heritage tourism” by writing position papers supported by research.

    Resources / Materials:
    -student journals
    -pens/pencils
    -paper
    -classroom board
    -copies of “India Dusts Off Colonial Past, Says Come to Goa” (one per student)

    Activities / Procedures:
    1. WARM-UP/DO NOW: Upon entering class, students respond to the following prompt in their journals (written on the board prior to class or copied into a handout for easier student access): “According to the Merriam-Webster dictionary (www.m-w.com), colonialism is defined as ‘control by one power over a dependent area or people,’ and imperialism is defined as ‘the policy, practice, or advocacy of extending the power and dominion of a nation especially by direct territorial acquisitions or by gaining indirect control over the political or economic life of other areas; broadly: the extension or imposition of power, authority, or influence.’ Brainstorm a list of countries that have been colonized or affected by imperialism, and another list of countries that have colonized other countries, or acted as imperialists in different parts of the world.” After a few minutes, allow students to share their responses. Create a chart on the board showing the two lists of countries, “Imperialistic Countries” and “Countries That Have Been Colonized.” Before adding a country to a list, ask students to state their justification for why a country should be included. Which countries should be on both lists? [Examples include China, Spain, India, and the United States] What’s the difference between colonizing a country and exercising imperial power? Why do you think one nation would want to take over another land or people? What might be some benefits that colonialists bring to a colonized nation?
    2. As a class, read and discuss the article “India Dusts Off Colonial Past, Says Come to Goa,” focusing on the following questions:
    a. What was the old name of the Indian state of Goa?
    b. How long was Goa under Portuguese rule?
    c. What hints of Portuguese culture still remain in Goa?
    d. What is helping Goans to learn or refresh the language of Portuguese?
    e. When were Goans considered to be Portuguese citizens?
    f. Why would a Goan want a Portuguese passport?
    g. Why are politicians who were initially against promoting Portuguese influences now supportive of the project?
    h. To what policies and political actions has the governor of Goa devoted his time?
    i. What history does the state director of tourism ignore in his statements? Why does this matter?

    3. Divide students into six groups. Explain that each group will research a different part of the world or country that was been affected by colonialism and imperialism, then create an oral presentation to communicate the group’s findings to the class. Assign each group an area or country, such as China, India, the West Indies, Latin America, Africa or Southeast Asia. Students should use the following questions to guide their research:
    -What area or country are you researching?
    -Where is it?
    -Why would a European nation want to colonize this area? (Consider natural resources, location, etc.)
    -When did this area become colonized?
    -Which countries colonized this area?
    -Who were the key people involved in this movement?
    -What were the effects of colonization?
    -What were the benefits and drawbacks of colonization in this area for the colonizers?
    -What were the benefits and drawbacks of colonization in this area for the indigenous people?
    Once research is completed, allow students to discuss and share their group’s findings, and ensure that each group member has a complete set of notes to take home. In addition, ask students to plan oral presentations to be done in a future class. While preparing these presentations, students should divide up their findings into separate portions for each group member to cover. For example, one student may cover “Pre-Imperialism,” another may cover “Days of Imperialism,” and so on.
    4. WRAP-UP/HOMEWORK: Individually, students will write position papers about whether or not “heritage tourism” would be a good idea for the people in their assigned part of the world. Students should consider the impact of imperialism on this country or region, and consider what tourists might see if they visited that part of the world. Encourage students to support their positions with facts gathered during class.

    Further Questions for Discussion:
    –Why is India trying to reinvent itself?
    –Why won’t government officials in Goa get involved in the issue of “heritage tourism”?
    –Who might be opposed to “heritage tourism”? Why?
    –Why isn’t the manor of the de Albuquerque family able to turn a profit?

    Evaluation / Assessment:
    Students will be evaluated based on initial journal responses, thoughtful participation in class discussions, thorough research in groups and accurate notes, and thoughtful completion of position papers based in fact.

    Vocabulary:
    teak, squatters, manor, monsoon, refurbishing, shunted, wending, overlay, surnames, coveted, ambivalence

    Extension Activities:
    1. Create a timeline illustrating the independence of a country in your assigned area of the world. When was independence granted? What was happening in this country at the time? What was happening in the imperialist country at this time? What people or organizations were involved in the imperialist movement? What attempts were made at independence, and how successful were these?
    2. Write a report about a famous explorer, such as Christopher Columbus, Vasco de Gama, Marco Polo, Francisco Pizarro, or Zheng He. What did they do? What impact did their travels have on the peoples with whom they interacted?
    3. Create a poster highlighting the history and culture of Portugal, including language, music, dance, religion, etc.

    Interdisciplinary Connections:
    Fine Arts- Create a photojournal illustrating the effects of colonialism in your homeland’s society. Find examples in street names, family names, historic sites, clothing, food and religion.
    Geography- Create a map of colonial India. Which countries had colonies in India? When were they occupied? How long did each occupation last?
    Language Arts- Read the “Poisonwood Bible” by Barbara Kingsolver or “Things Fall Apart” by Chinua Achebe and write a brief report explaining the effects of colonialism on an African nation.

    Other Information on the Web:
    Goa Research Net (http://www.geocities.com/Athens/Forum/1503/) provides information about research and news relating to Goa.
    Indian History: A Passage Through Time (http://www.historyofindia.com/) allows you to take a virtual tour through 5000 years of India’s history.
    InvestIndia (http://www.investindia.com/newsite/index.htm) provides a comprehensive guide to the history and culture of India.

    Academic Content Standards:
    Grades 6-8
    Historical Understanding Standard 2- Understands the historical perspective. Benchmarks: Analyzes the influence specific ideas and beliefs had on a period of history; Analyzes the effects specific decisions had on history; Knows different types of primary and secondary sources and the motives, interests, and bias expressed in them
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’6-8’, ‘hu2’)
    World History Standard 26- Understands how the transoceanic interlinking of all major regions of the world between 1450 and 1600 led to global transformations. Benchmarks: Understands the impact of the exploratory and commercial expeditions in the 15th and 16th centuries; Understands cultural interaction between various societies in the late 15th and 16th centuries
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’6-8’, ‘wh7’)
    World History Standard 33- Understands major global trends from 1450 to 1770. Benchmark: Understands patterns of social and cultural continuity in various societies
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’6-8’, ‘wh8’)
    Geography Standard 6- Understands that culture and experience influence people’s perceptions of places and regions. Benchmark: Knows the ways in which culture influences the perception of places and regions
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’6-8’, ‘geo2’)
    Geography Standard 12- Understands the patterns of human settlement and their causes. Benchmarks: Knows the causes and consequences of urbanization; Knows the similarities and differences in various settlement patterns of the world; Knows ways in which both the landscape and society change as a consequence of shifting from a dispersed to a concentrated settlement form
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’6-8’, ‘geo4’)
    Language Arts Standard 7- Demonstrates competence in the general skills and strategies for reading a variety of informational texts. Benchmarks: Applies reading skills and strategies to a variety of informational texts; Summarizes and paraphrases complex, explicit hierarchic structures in informational texts; Uses new information to adjust and extend personal knowledge base
    (CTSS – ‘english’, ’6-8’, ’7’)
    Grades 9-12
    Historical Understanding Standard 2- Understands the historical perspective. Benchmarks: Analyzes the influences specific ideas and beliefs had on a period of history and specifies how events might have been different in the absence of those ideas and beliefs; Understands that change and continuity are equally probable and natural; Evaluates the validity and credibility of different historical interpretations
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’9-12’, ‘hu2’)
    World History Standard 26- Understands how the transoceanic interlinking of all major regions of the world between 1450 and 1600 led to global transformations. Benchmarks: Understands features of Portuguese overseas trade and exploration; Understands significant social, economic, political, and cultural features of European society (particularly Spain and Portugal) that stimulated exploration and conquest overseas; Understands the consequences of Portuguese military conflicts and interaction with other cultures; Knows the dynamics of the encomienda system of colonial government and labor, and how this compares to European manorial systems
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’9-12’, ‘wh7’)
    World History Standard 33- Understands major global trends from 1450 to 1770. Benchmark: Understands the emergence of capitalism in India and Europe
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’9-12’, ‘wh8’)
    Geography Standard 6- Understands that culture and experience influence people’s perceptions of places and regions. Benchmark: Knows ways in which people’s changing views of places and regions reflect cultural change
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’9-12’, ‘geo2’)
    Geography Standard 12- Understands the patterns of human settlement and their causes. Benchmarks: Understands how the functions of cities today differ from those of towns and villages and cities in earlier times; Knows the shape of cities in the United States and factors that influence urban morphology
    (CTSS – ‘social’, ’9-12’, ‘geo4’)
    Language Arts Standard 7- Demonstrates competence in the general skills and strategies for reading a variety of informational texts. Benchmarks: Applies reading skills and strategies to a variety of informational texts; Summarizes and paraphrases complex, implicit hierarchic structures in informational texts, including the relationships among the concepts and details in those structures; Uses new information from texts to clarify or refine understanding of academic concepts
    (CTSS – ‘english’, ’9-12’, ’7’)

    This lesson plan may be used to address the academic standards listed above. These standards are drawn from Content Knowledge: A Compendium of Standards and Benchmarks for K-12 Education; 3rd and 4th Editions and have been provided courtesy of the Mid-continent Research for Education and Learning in Aurora, Colorado.
